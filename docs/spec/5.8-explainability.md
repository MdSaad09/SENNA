# 5.8 Explainability layer — what is explained, how, and how to say it

Extracted from the SENNA Master Implementation Specification v3.0 (7 July 2026) — docs/spec/source/SENNA_Master_Spec_v3.pdf, Part 5. The PDF is the authority; report divergences, never patch them here.

## 5.8.1 What a SHAP value means here (the mechanistic statement)

For one decision at one lap: the policy maps the 32-dimension state to a preference score (logit) for each action. The SHAP value of feature i is that feature's average marginal contribution to the chosen action's score, relative to a background distribution of typical race states, averaged over orderings (Shapley axioms). Positive pushes toward the chosen action; negative pushes away. It is an attribution of the trained function's sensitivity — it is not a causal claim about the world, and the write-up says so.

## 5.8.2 Pipeline (replaces v2 Section 6.5 — see F-25)

- Distill: log 200k (state, action-probability) pairs from evaluation rollouts; fit one gradient-boosted tree model per action head (XGBoost, multi-class) to imitate the policy.
- Report fidelity: top-1 agreement between surrogate and policy on held-out states — target at least 90% per head; also report mean KL. The fidelity number is printed on the dashboard next to every explanation. If fidelity misses 90%, deepen the surrogate before shipping explanations.
- Explain: exact TreeSHAP on the surrogate for every logged decision — fast enough for all evaluation races and for per-lap live cards (computed in the worker, streamed in the agent:decision event).
- Cross-check: GradientExplainer attributions on the true network for 500 sampled decisions; report the rank correlation with surrogate attributions as an integrity statistic in Chapter 7.
- Background set: 1,024 states stratified across circuits, race phases and track statuses; the background choice is documented because attributions depend on it.
- Counterfactual flip probes: for headline decisions, sweep one feature across its physical range with all else fixed and record where the policy's action flips ("stays out until tyre_age_norm = 0.42, i.e. about 17 laps"). Model-faithful, cheap, and the most persuasive line on a decision card.

## 5.8.3 The decision card (the recruiter-facing artefact)

> | Field | Example content |
> |---|---|
> | Decision | Lap 28 - PIT for Medium + attack out-lap (probability 0.83) |
> | Top drivers (TreeSHAP, fidelity 93%) | tyre grip est. -0.31 · gap behind 0.22 · ahead tyre age delta 0.19 · SOC 0.11 · lap progress 0.07 |
> | Counterfactual flip | Would stay out if grip were above 0.58 (about 3 laps fresher) or if the car behind were more than 4.1 s back |
> | Plain-language line | "Worn tyres plus an undercut threat from behind; the model banked energy last lap to attack the out-lap." |
> | Honesty footer | Attribution of the trained policy via a 93%-faithful surrogate; not a causal claim. |

How to present it to a non-technical recruiter without losing accuracy: "For every decision, we show the five inputs that moved the model most, and the exact conditions under which it would have decided differently. The explanation comes from a simplified twin of the network that agrees with it 93% of the time — and that agreement number is always on screen." Every clause of that sentence is literally true of the pipeline above.
