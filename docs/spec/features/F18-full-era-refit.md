# F18 — Full-era refit and revalidation

Depends on: F02, F16 (both Done)
Spec anchors: 5.3.1, 5.5.3, E-10, E-12, E-14, T5, A-09, M1–M11

> Extends master spec v3.0 rather than implementing a row of it. The anchors are the
> contracts the refit must not break — the cleaning rules, the joint regression's
> identification discipline, the frozen calibration split — while every fitted number
> underneath them is expected to move.

## Correction, 11 August 2026 (during /ship)

**The diagnosis below is wrong in its mechanism, and the record should show both.** It was
written from the reproduction table, which is correct and still stands: a 2024+2025-scoped
fit reproduces the committed constants exactly, and the full era moves Bahrain by about
0.9 s/lap. The inference from it — that only two seasons had been ingested — is not.

The pre-refit `configs/physics_params.yaml` carries its own watermark,
`generated_at: "seasons 2022-2026, round 6"`, rendered from the same circuit index the fit
ran over. It also carries a `hazards` block for `french_grand_prix`, a race held only in
2022, with per-circuit race counts identical to the post-refit file. Every declared season
was in the store when that file was written.

What was actually missing was one layer down: 2022 and 2023 laps reached the hazard,
pit-loss and energy modules and reached **none** of the joint pace fits. So base pace was
fitted on 1958 laps of an available 3705 while the file's own watermark claimed the full
span — the scope was not merely unrecorded, it was recorded wrongly, which is a sharper
statement of the defect than the one below.

Consequence for the deliverables: the season-coverage gate is still correct and still
worth having, but it would have passed the defective run without a word. The gate that
matches this defect is `assert_pace_season_coverage`, added during the build, and the
guard that actually failed on `main` is the byte-identity round-trip test.

## Goal
`configs/physics_params.yaml` was fitted while only the 2024 and 2025 seasons reached the
pace fits, though all of 2022–2025 were in the store and in the frozen A-09 snapshot (see
the correction above). The constants were never refitted, and the season scope that
produced them is misrecorded — `configs/ingest.yaml` declares `seasons: [2022, 2023, 2024,
2025, 2026]` and the file's watermark claims the same, neither of which is what the
regression saw.

Reproduced exactly, at Bahrain, era 2024:

| Season scope | n_laps | t0_s | sigma_driver_s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 + 2025 | 1958 | 98.597 | 0.5653 |
| **committed `physics_params.yaml`** | **1958** | **98.597** | **0.5653** |
| all 2022–2025 (what `make fit` does today) | 3705 | 99.465 | 0.6571 |

Two consequences. The reproducibility pack does not round-trip: restore the snapshot,
follow `docs/freeze.md`, run `make fit`, and the physics differs from what the repo ships
by roughly 0.9 s/lap on base pace. And the fitted layer is using half the data it has, for
no stated reason — 2022 through 2025 are one regulation era, which is why they share an
`era` value in the first place.

After F18 the constants are fitted on every season of the era, the whole downstream chain
is regenerated from them, and a test fails the build if the committed constants ever again
disagree with what the frozen data produces.

## Out of scope
- **Driver identity (F17).** It depends on this: its whole premise is that driver effects come from the same regression that produced the committed `t0_s` and `sigma_driver_s`. It cannot be built until that regression is the one the simulator runs.
- **Any change to the physics model itself.** F18 changes what data the existing fitters see, not what they compute. No new term, no new form, no new constant beyond the coverage gate.
- **Re-deciding the era boundary.** 2022–2025 share an era because the regulations did; that is `configs/ingest.yaml`'s `first_2026_season`, already sourced, and F18 does not reopen it.
- Physics uncertainty (F19), explanation language (F20), observation changes (F21).

## Constants & configs
F18 introduces no physics constants — it regenerates them. The single new number is a
gate:

| Name | Value | Origin | Home |
|---|---|---|---|
| `require_full_season_coverage` | `true` | **operational** — a correctness gate, not a tunable; sized by the defect it prevents | `configs/fits.yaml` |

Every regenerated value keeps the provenance tag its fitter already emits, through
`params_writer`. The diff on `physics_params.yaml` will be large and that is the point:
it should be reviewed as evidence, not skimmed.

## Tests first
- **`test_refit_reproduces_the_committed_params`** *(slow, DB-backed)* — the headline test. `make fit` over the frozen snapshot renders byte-identically to `configs/physics_params.yaml`. Red before the refit, green after, and red forever after if anyone refits on partial data again.
- **`test_a_partial_ingest_refuses_to_write_params`** — an ingest missing a declared season raises rather than writing. The defect, pinned.
- **`test_every_era_2024_season_reaches_the_fit`** — the joint-pace input for a sample circuit contains laps from all four seasons, asserted by season not by count.
- **`test_params_still_render_deterministically`** — `params_writer`'s existing guarantee, re-checked on the new file.
- The whole existing suite must stay green. Tests that assert *specific fitted values* will fail and must be updated to the new numbers deliberately, one at a time, never by bulk find-and-replace — each one is a claim about the physics and deserves a look.

## Edge cases handled
- **E-12 completeness** — the new coverage gate is the same idea as the ingest completeness assertion, applied one layer later.
- **E-14 frozen split** — `_refuse_validation_rounds` stays in the path; the 2026 calibration rounds are untouched, so held-out validation data stays held out.
- **E-10 identification** — the VIF gate is re-run on the larger sample. A circuit whose fuel/evolution attribution was unidentified on two seasons may become identified on four; that is a result, and it must be reported, not hidden.
- **T5 transfer** — degradation's `min_drivers` / `min_stints` gates see more data, so fewer circuits should fall back to the cluster transfer. Also a result.
- **A-09** — the pack round-trips after this, which is the whole point.

## Notes for the plan session

**This feature's output is numbers, and all of them will be different.** The temptation
will be to treat the `physics_params.yaml` diff as noise and move on. It is the evidence.
Two things must be read out of it before anything downstream runs: which circuits changed
identification status (E-10), and which compounds stopped needing T5 transfer. Both are
reportable findings and both belong in the dissertation.

**The F14 headline will move and may get worse.** M1 was +2.08 places with CI [1.71,
2.48]. More data is better physics, not necessarily a better-looking result, and the
honest position is to report the new number whatever it is. Plan for the possibility that
the story changes; it is still a stronger dissertation than one whose constants cannot be
reproduced from its own data pack.

**Sequence matters and the chain is long.** `fit -> synth -> circuits -> train/headline ->
explain -> finetune -> eval -> sensitivity -> report`. Every stage reads the previous one's
artifacts, so a mistake at `fit` is only visible at `eval` hours later. Check the fit diff
before spending compute on anything downstream.

**Compute is not the constraint.** Ablations are 4M steps per arm, four arms, two seeds —
32M steps, about an hour of env time at the measured throughput, plus SB3 overhead. The
expensive part is artifact management and the results rewrite, not GPU hours.

**Keep the old results.** The previous headline model, its fidelity badge and F14's
numbers stay in INDEX beside the new ones. An examiner asking "did the results change when
you fixed the data" deserves a straight answer with both figures, and a viva answer of
"the physics improved and here is exactly what moved" is stronger than a clean slate.
