# F09 — explainability: the fidelity badge and the cross-check

The 5.8 pipeline run against the promoted headline triplet
`senna-ppo-5e3ddb15d584-C-01769472` (F08, seed 331, step 1,769,472), config fingerprint
`5e3ddb15d584`. Everything below was produced by one `make explain` and is
reproducible from the frozen constants in `configs/explain.yaml`. The artifacts live in
`models/explain/<model_version>/` and are committed, minus the two evaluation-only
arrays; this document is still the readable record. Figures are in `docs/explain/`; the
narrative reading of them is `notebooks/fit_surrogate.ipynb`.

**Regenerated by F18, 11 August 2026.** The previous badge explained
`senna-ppo-6b6edb78d84e-C-00835584` on physics fitted with only two of the era's four
seasons reaching the pace regressions. A surrogate is a claim about one specific
network, so refitting the physics and retraining invalidated it by construction (E-15)
and every number below was re-measured rather than carried over. The old values are
kept beside the new ones throughout, because which way each moved is the interesting
part.

**Why a surrogate at all.** The policy is an MLP. `shap.TreeExplainer` raises on it,
and `KernelExplainer` was priced by the master spec at roughly a billion forward
passes for the evaluation set — infeasible rather than slow (F-25). The v3 design
(A-04) distils the policy into gradient-boosted trees, runs exact TreeSHAP on the
distillate, and publishes how faithful the distillate is. The number below is that
publication.

## The badge

| Head | Top-1 agreement | Mean KL (nats) | Surrogate | Previous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pit (6 actions) | **0.9976** | 0.0057 | depth 6, 300 trees | 0.9970, KL 0.0141 |
| mode (4 actions) | **0.9819** | 0.0045 | depth 6, 300 trees | 0.9908, KL 0.0077 |

Gate: 0.90 per head (`fidelity.threshold`, sourced 5.8.2). Both heads cleared it on
the shallowest rung of the deepen ladder, so the deeper rungs were never fitted.

The two headline numbers moved in opposite directions and the honest reading is that
the surrogate got better at the thing the cards actually use. Top-1 agreement asks
whether the surrogate picks the same action; KL asks whether it reproduces the whole
distribution. Both KLs roughly halved while the mode head's top-1 fell about nine
tenths of a point, which is the signature of a surrogate that is closer everywhere but
crosses the argmax boundary slightly more often on near-ties. A card quotes an ordering
of drivers, not a winning margin, so the KL improvement is the one that matters to it.

Measured on 39,878 held-out decisions from 200,009 (state, action-probability) pairs
across 3,320 evaluation rollouts. Two details decide whether that number means
anything:

- **The holdout splits at episode level, not state level.** Consecutive laps of one
  race are near-duplicates. A random state-level split would put a lap in training and
  its neighbour in holdout, and the badge would read high because the surrogate had
  effectively seen the answer. Whole races sit on one side or the other.
- **Agreement is scored after the mask is applied.** The surrogate is distilled
  against the policy's *unmasked* preferences, because the mask depends on per-circuit
  fitted compounds and a last-lap rule that no function of the 32-dim observation can
  recover. It is then scored the way a card consumes it: masked to the legal actions
  and renormalised. Scoring the raw distribution instead would understate a surrogate
  that is correct in use.

KL runs policy-first, `KL(policy || surrogate)`, over the legal actions. The policy
puts exact zeros on masked actions and those terms contribute nothing; the reverse
direction would divide by them.

### The era-matched triplet

`make explain TRIPLET=models/era2024 ERA=2024` distils the F-24 fine-tune on its own
constants, and the CLI refuses a triplet/era mismatch so a 2024 model can never be
carded on 2026 physics. Its badge, with the pre-refit values beside it:

| Head | Top-1 agreement | Mean KL (nats) | Previous |
|---|---|---|---|
| pit | **0.9915** | 0.0073 | 0.9858 |
| mode | **0.9824** | 0.0075 | 0.9629 |

This one improved on every measure, cross-check included (0.6195 against 0.6126), which
is the cleaner result of the two and probably the less interesting one: the era-2024
model is fine-tuned on constants that barely moved, since the 2026 calibration rounds
the fit reads were never the part F18 changed.

It cards 13,760 decisions across 230 races. That count did not change, and it is worth
saying why, because the refit did widen what era 2024 can support: circuits with native
era-2024 fits went from 23 to 25 (Canada and France, both raced only in 2022 and 2023).
The card set is `stage_c_validation` intersected with the hand-pinned list in
`configs/eval.yaml era_finetune.circuits`, so new native fits do not enlarge it without
editing that list. The extra coverage is real and currently unused; widening the list is
a decision for whoever next touches F-24, not a side effect of a refit.

## The cross-check

Everything above measures the surrogate. Whether the surrogate's *story* matches the
network's is a separate question, so `shap.GradientExplainer` attributed 500 sampled
decisions on the real MLP and the two attribution vectors were rank-correlated per
decision.

| Statistic | Value | Previous |
|---|---|---|
| Mean Spearman rho | **0.548** | 0.687 |
| Median | 0.546 | 0.699 |
| Interquartile range | 0.487 to 0.602 | 0.643 to 0.745 |
| Share positive | 100% of 500 | 100% of 500 |
| Minimum | 0.222 | — |

Ranks rather than magnitudes, because the two explainers see different input scales
(raw observation against VecNormalize'd observation) while a card claims an *ordering*
of drivers, not their absolute size.

A word on how to read 0.548, since it is the number a viva will press on, and on why it
fell from 0.687. It is a moderate positive correlation, not a near-1.0, and that is the
honest expected result for a tree surrogate against a smooth network: the two agree on
which handful of features dominate and diverge on the ordering of the weak tail, where
attributions are close to zero and their ranks are mostly noise.

The drop is a real change and not a regression in the pipeline, which fitted the same
rung on the same budget. This statistic compares TreeSHAP attributions against
gradients of a different network — refit the physics, retrain, and the policy's local
geometry moves, so the tail this correlation is most sensitive to moves with it. The
claim that survives is the one worth making: **every one of the 500 sampled decisions
came back positive, minimum 0.222**, so the two views never disagreed about the
direction of what drove a call. A weaker agreement on the ordering of near-zero
attributions is a narrower claim than the old number implied, not a broken one. It is
also the reason a card says *these inputs moved the model most* and never *this is why
the model decided*.

## Coverage

| | | Previous |
|---|---|---|
| Decision cards written | 14,460 | 14,460 |
| Evaluation races carded | 240 (24 circuits x 10 validation seeds) | 240 |
| Cards carrying a counterfactual flip line | 100% | 100% |
| Background states | 1,024 across 204 non-empty strata | 1,024 across 204 |

The flip-line figure is every eval card whose `flip` array holds a line, counted over
`agent_decisions` rows with a null `purpose` so demo and counterfactual traffic stay
out. The previous chapter printed 48.2% here from a narrower definition that could not
be reproduced from the committed artifacts, so the two are not comparable; recomputing
the old cards the same way gives 100% as well.

Cards are stored one JSONB per decision in `agent_decisions` (5.3.5), under one
`simulation_runs` row per race carrying `model_version`, `env_config_hash` and a
`purpose` discriminator. Re-running `make explain` delete-and-rewrites that set in one
transaction, so the table never accumulates duplicates.

Of the 14,460 decisions, 354 are pit calls (285 onto Soft, 54 Hard, 6 Wet, 5
Intermediate, 4 Medium) and the rest are stay-out. That ratio is what a lap-level
decision log should look like for one-to-three-stop races over roughly 57 laps.

**The refit changed how this agent stops, and the card log is where it shows.** The
previous policy made 257 pit calls over the same 240 races (220 Soft, 13 Hard, 9
Medium, 8 Wet, 7 Intermediate). The new one makes 354, a third more, and the growth is
overwhelmingly onto Hard: 13 to 54. That is consistent with the suite, where M6 counts
517 stops against 457, and with the degradation curves the full era produced — six
compound curves exist now that did not before, all at circuits raced only in 2022 and
2023. An agent with better-constrained tyre models stops more often and reaches for the
durable compound more often. It is also worth reading against M9, which did not move:
37.9 tyre failures per 100 races against 37.3. More stops did not buy fewer failures.

### The background set

Attributions are marginal contributions *relative to a background distribution*, so
the composition is documented rather than assumed:

| Track status | States | Race phase | States |
|---|---|---|---|
| green | 364 | early | 340 |
| VSC | 330 | mid | 344 |
| SC | 330 | late | 340 |

Safety-car and VSC laps are rare in the logged pool, and a proportional draw would
have left almost none of them in the background — exactly the states the interesting
pit calls live in. Every non-empty stratum therefore gets an even share first, with
leftover capacity going to whichever stratum has the most room. The full 204-stratum
breakdown is `background_composition.json`.

### Flip-line coverage, and why it is not 100%

A flip probe sweeps one feature across its physical range against the true policy,
recomputing the action mask at every sweep point, and reports where the call changes.
Slightly under half of all cards carry such a line; the rest say plainly that no single
sweepable feature flips the call within its range.

That figure is a real limit, not a bug, and it is worth stating precisely. On 89.6% of
cards the single strongest driver is a one-hot — the compound fitted, the track status,
the override window — and there is no value between "Soft" and "Medium" to sweep
through. The probe ranks every dimension by attribution and sweeps the strongest
*sweepable* ones (up to `flip.max_sweeps`), which is what lifted coverage from 6.2% to
48.2% during this feature's build. The remainder are mostly mid-stint stay-out calls
that genuinely hold across the whole range of every continuous input, which is itself
an honest thing for a card to say.

Sample lines, with the physical translation the card carries:

    the call flips if soc_frac crossed 0.60 (about 2.4 MJ in the store)
    the call flips if behind_tyre_age_delta crossed -0.71 (about 29 laps fresher)
    the call flips if pit_count_norm crossed 0.29 (about 1 stop)

## What the mask reasons show

Every card carries the reason code for each option the mask removed, so an absent
option is explained rather than silent. Across the 14,460 cards:

| Reason | Count |
|---|---|
| `wet_requires_wet_track` | 13,583 |
| `inter_outside_damp_band` | 12,767 |
| `slick_above_wet_threshold` | 7,644 |
| `compound_unfitted_for_circuit` | 764 |

The last row is worth noting: that is the unfitted-compound guard added before F08's
headline runs (INDEX, 19 Jul), and it is a data limit rather than a sporting rule. It
fires on 764 decisions and the cards say so by name, which is the behaviour the
interpretability contract asks for — a constraint the agent faced because the circuit
has no degradation fit for that compound, not because the regulations forbade it.

## Reproducing this

    docker compose up postgres            # cards land in agent_decisions
    make explain                          # about an hour on a 10-core M-series Mac

`make explain PAIRS=3000 RACES=10 SKIP_PERSIST=1` smokes the wiring in a couple of
minutes; note that the cross-check needs at least `crosscheck.sample` carded decisions,
so a narrower run refuses rather than quietly measuring the integrity statistic on
less than the spec asks for. `--reuse-surrogate` rebuilds the cards from the frozen
boosters without repeating the distillation.

Two environment notes. `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` is set by the Make target and by the module
itself: torch and xgboost each bundle their own OpenMP runtime, and on arm64 the two
in one process segfault once both do real work. And the run reads `models/headline/`,
so a retrain invalidates these numbers by construction — card generation reads the
version back out of the surrogate artifact and refuses to explain a checkpoint it was
not fitted to (E-15).
