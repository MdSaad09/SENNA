0About this document

This is the design and implementation record for the whole system: what was built, what each decision rejected, and where the evidence for every claim lives. It is written to feed the dissertation's design and implementation chapters, and to let a reader who has never seen the repository understand why the code looks the way it does.

Three conventions run through it.

Every claim points somewhere. Design decisions cite the file that implements them; numbers cite the artifact that produced them. Where a figure appears in prose it can be found in configs/, docs/results/, or a named module. Nothing here is asserted from memory.

Identifiers are the shared vocabulary. The master specification (v3.0, 7 July 2026, docs/spec/source/) assigns identifiers to its findings and decisions, and the code cites them in comments: F-xx audit findings, E-xx edge cases, R-xx removals from the previous design, A-xx additions, T1–T8 synthetic-data transformations, M1–M11 evaluation metrics, AR-xx assumptions, OQ-xx open questions. Appendix A glosses the ones that recur. A reader can grep any of them and land on the code that honours it.

The failures are in here too. Six of the eleven evaluation metrics missed their targets, one physics validation failed outright, and several designs were replaced after they shipped. Those outcomes are reported in the same voice as the successes, with their causes, because a system that only documents its wins has documented nothing an examiner can check.

1The problem

1.1Race strategy as a decision problem

A Formula 1 race is a sequential decision problem under uncertainty with a small action space and a brutal credit-assignment horizon. Once per lap a strategist chooses whether to pit and onto which tyre, and how to spend the car's electrical energy. Roughly sixty such decisions decide a result, the reward arrives once at the flag, and the state that matters — tyre condition, fuel load, energy in the store, the gaps to the cars around — evolves under events nobody controls: safety cars, rain, failures, other teams' stops.

That shape suits reinforcement learning. It also suits it badly in one specific way: the outcome is dominated by luck. Quantifying that turned out to be one of this project's more interesting results (§6.4).

1.2Why the 2026 regulations

The 2026 Formula 1 technical regulations change the strategic problem more than any ruleset since 2014, and they are widely misreported. The three facts that matter:

  • The MGU-K delivers 350 kW, roughly triple the previous era's 120 kW, with power held flat to 290 km/h and tapering to zero at 355 km/h. The 400 kW figure that circulates is the internal combustion engine.
  • The energy store is a 4 MJ state-of-charge window, not an 8 MJ battery. The regulation constrains the difference between maximum and minimum state of charge (FIA 2026 Technical Regulations Art 5.4.9, carried into the consolidated Section C as Art C5.2.9), which is a window rather than a capacity.
  • There is no per-lap deployment cap. The 2014–2025 regulations capped energy from the store to the MGU-K at 4 MJ per lap; the 2026 text does not carry that limit. The era is recovery-limited instead, through a per-circuit harvest ceiling.

That last point is the one that reshapes strategy. When deployment is capped, energy management is a scheduling problem inside a fixed budget. When recovery is capped and deployment is not, energy becomes a stock the driver can spend down and must earn back, and lifting to harvest costs lap time now to buy lap time later. An agent that can price that trade is doing something a 2024-era agent never had to.

An earlier version of this project's own specification had these facts wrong — it carried the 4 MJ/lap cap, an 8 MJ store and 400 kW as the electric figure. The v3 specification is the corrected one, and the corrections are the reason several subsystems look the way they do (§2.2).

1.4The claims

Four things this system does that a template project would not:

  1. Corrected 2026 physics, from primary sources, with the corrections traceable to the audit that found them.
  2. Regulatory compliance by construction — illegal actions are masked out of the policy's distribution rather than penalised in reward, which turns compliance from a convergence property into an architectural one, measured at zero violations across 480 evaluation races.
  3. Sim-to-real validation of the synthetic layer against held-out real 2026 rounds, under a split frozen before any fit existed — which is how the synthetic layer's failure became a measurable result rather than an unexamined assumption.
  4. A variance decomposition separating what the event draw explains from what the decisions explain: 75.8% of finishing-position variance is the draw, a conservative lower bound.

2Requirements and constraints

2.1The specification as contract

The master specification is treated as a contract rather than a starting point. Its Part 5 was split into one markdown file per section under docs/spec/ so a working session loads only what it needs; Parts 1, 3 and 4 stay in the source PDF and are cited by identifier. Where a feature specification and the master specification disagree, the master wins and the disagreement is recorded in the feature's plan rather than silently resolved. Around thirty such divergences and interpretation flags are recorded across the sixteen plans; Appendix D lists the ones that shaped the architecture.

The specification also carries an audit of the project's earlier design — thirty findings (F-01–F-30), twelve removals (R-01–R-12) and twelve additions (A-01–A-12). Those identifiers are not decoration. R-04 (the Ergast API shut down) is enforced by a test that greps every tracked file under the code directories for the dead service's name — documentation is deliberately out of scope, because the specification itself names it. F-25 (SHAP cannot be run directly on the policy network) is enforced by a function signature that only accepts a tree booster. The audit is executable.

2.2The interpretability contract

The project's one non-negotiable requirement, stated in the specification's Part 0.4 and in the repository's root CLAUDE.md: an inline constant in code is a defect. Every number lives in configs/*.yaml with a comment naming its origin, and there are exactly four legal origins:

  • sourced — a document backs it; the comment cites the assumptions-register row (AR-xx) or the specification section.
  • fitted — a calibration notebook produced it; the comment names the notebook.
  • stylised — an explicit presentation choice; the comment says so and the UI labels it on screen.
  • operational — an engineering default (a timeout, a page size, a poll cadence); the comment states its sizing rationale. This fourth origin was added during the serving feature rather than stretching "stylised" to cover a 600-second job timeout.

Twenty-one configuration files carry 4,400 lines under this rule. The generated fits artifact alone (configs/physics_params.yaml) is 2,267 lines holding 1,765 leaf values, each with a per-line origin comment; 1,763 of them name the notebook that produced the value, and the other two are the artifact's own data watermark and random seed. A null means "fit me": senna/config.py raises NullConstantError rather than letting a placeholder flow into a simulation.

The contract's real test is not whether numbers live in yaml. It is whether an examiner pointing at any number on screen can reach its origin in one step. That is why the frontend carries the same rule (frontend/src/config/constants.ts tags every value with the same four origins), why the dashboard's dev overlay traces every displayed figure back to the API response that produced it, and why stylised visuals — the interpolated car motion, the exaggerated elevation, the tyre-temperature axis — say so in words on screen rather than in a comment nobody reads.

3Architecture

3.1The pipeline

The system is a directed pipeline with one loop back for evaluation. Each stage's output is an artifact on disk, which is what makes any stage re-runnable without the ones before it.

Three properties of this shape are load-bearing.

Artifacts, not memory. Every arrow crossing a stage boundary is a file: a yaml of constants, a model triplet, a GLB mesh, a JSON store row. A stage can be re-run in isolation and its output diffed. This is what makes make report a pure function of the repository — it reads only the committed store, never a model or a database.

One direction, one exception. The evaluation suite is the only stage that reads back into the middle of the pipeline: it loads the frozen model, rolls it in the environment, and reads the synthetic layer's own validation summary for the sim-to-real metric. Nothing else loops.

Provenance travels with the data. A model version string embeds the hash of the eleven configuration files the run was reproducible from (senna-ppo-<hash12>-<stage>-<step>); an evaluation store row carries that version, the config hash, the seed-manifest fingerprint and the git commit. The chain is checkable from a clone, and make verify checks it.

3.2Runtime topology

Serving is five processes, and the split between them is a design decision rather than an operational convenience.

browser ──HTTP/WS──► api (FastAPI, uvicorn)
                       │  validates, records, enqueues — never simulates
                       ├──► postgres  (runs, lap frames, decision cards, ingested data)
                       └──► redis     (RQ queue, result cache, pub/sub)
                                │
                              worker (rq SimpleWorker)
                                │  runs whole races with the frozen triplet,
                                └──► postgres (frames + cards, one transaction)

FastAPI runs every request handler and every open WebSocket on one asyncio event loop. A 70-lap simulation inside a handler would freeze every connected stream and the health endpoint with it, so simulations run in the RQ worker and never in the request path (F-26). Even yaml reads leave the loop through run_in_threadpool.

The WebSocket is not a live feed. The worker persists the whole race in one transaction before any client connects; the socket is a playback clock over stored rows (E-16). That makes pause, scrub and reconnect ordinary reads — a seek is a list index move, and a dropped connection needs no recovery because the next socket replays from the same store.

The worker runs RQ's SimpleWorker rather than the default forking worker. The default forks a work horse per job, which discards the model cache and re-imports torch for every race; one process, loaded once, is the right shape for a serving worker whose crash isolation belongs to the platform's restart policy.

3.3Artifact classes

Three kinds of artifact, three different disciplines:

Configuration (configs/*.yaml, committed) — every constant with provenance. Some are hand-written and sourced; some are generated by a fitting battery and byte- deterministic, so git diff after make fit is the change detector.

Models (models/, mostly gitignored) — the frozen triplet is weights plus VecNormalize statistics plus a manifest, saved as one unit with both artifacts' SHA-256 stamped into the manifest before the directory is swapped in (F-23). Weights without their normalisation statistics receive raw observations where they expect z-scores, and nothing throws — the triplet exists so that failure cannot happen silently. The promoted headline, the era-matched fine-tune and both SHAP surrogates are committed despite the gitignore, because a clean clone must be able to serve the demo.

Evidence stores (docs/results/*/store/*.json, committed) — one JSON per metric, sorted keys, fixed indent, no timestamps anywhere, each wrapped in a provenance envelope. A rerun that changed nothing produces identical bytes, which is what makes "deterministic given the run store" checkable rather than asserted.

4Cross-cutting design principles

Seven ideas recur across every subsystem. They are the intellectual spine of the project, and most of them were learned the hard way.

4.1Structural guarantees over learned ones

Wherever a property can be made impossible to violate, it is, rather than being discouraged after the fact.

The clearest case is regulatory compliance. The earlier design penalised illegal actions in the reward. That only discourages them in expectation after training converges, so early in training and on rare states illegal actions still execute — and no compliance percentage can ever be claimed, only measured. SENNA masks them: MaskablePPO applies the mask inside the action distribution, so an illegal action has probability zero in both rollout and update. Compliance moves from a hope to a property of the architecture. The measured result is 0 coerced actions across 480 evaluation races.

The same instinct recurs: the reserved test seed bank is not "documented as off-limits", it is behind a loader that refuses every caller but the evaluation feature; the training environment cannot select the held-out rival band because the constructor raises; the database wipe guard refuses any database not named *_test, resolving the name the way the driver does because a ?database= query parameter once bypassed a path-only check.

The rejected alternative is always kept where it can be measured. The penalty architecture survives as an ablation arm reachable by a constructor flag, and it scores about 1.8 points below the best-performing arm and half a point below the matched control (§6.3). The choice is defended with a number rather than an argument.

4.2Provenance is a data structure, not a comment convention

§2.2 describes the contract. What makes it hold under pressure is that provenance is carried in the artifacts rather than reconstructed later.

The fits battery emits (value, origin) pairs and a hand-rolled YAML writer renders key: value # origin per line, because pyyaml cannot write per-line comments and the comments are the point. Grade-C assumptions propagate transitively: a 2024 circuit whose mass attribution came through the 2026 pool is tagged "grade C via AR-10 fuel — through the pooled k_m" even though its own era's fuel figure is verified. Ninety-one lines in the generated artifact carry that flag.

Evaluation rows carry an envelope — model versions, config hash, seed fingerprint, git commit, and a scope block. Scope exists because without it a two-circuit smoke row and a 480-race row are indistinguishable on disk, and the report would print the smoke number under the full run's claim.

4.3Store the flagged superset, filter at the point of use

The cleaning contract keeps every ingested row and stores derived flags rather than writing a cleaned subset. In-laps and out-laps poison a pace fit but are exactly what a pit-timing fit needs; non-green laps poison both but are what an event-rate fit is made of. Cleaning at write time makes every cleaning decision irreversible, so ingest flags and fits filter at query time.

The same principle governs how misses are reported. The synthetic layer's held-out validation failed — 0 of 4 circuits within the KS target — and the failure ships with its mechanism attached rather than being tuned away. Tuning a constant on the held-out rounds to close that gap is precisely the contamination the frozen split exists to prevent, so the honest move is to publish the miss and name the cause.

4.4Determinism, and the difference between deterministic and pure

Byte-determinism is bought deliberately: a total ORDER BY on every fitting query (float summation order changes the last digit), sorted keys at every level of every generated artifact, and generated-at watermarks that describe the data rather than reading a clock.

The evaluation report went one step further and had to learn the difference between deterministic and pure. Rendering the same store twice in one process gives identical bytes; that test passed while the report still embedded the filesystem path its figures happened to land in. The fix was to make figure links always name the committed layout, and the test that catches it renders in three separate subprocesses under different PYTHONHASHSEED values, because Python's hash salt is fixed for a process's lifetime and the in-process check cannot see set-iteration drift.

4.5Layering and the hot loop

The environment's step() is called tens of millions of times per training run, so the constraints on it are structural: every constant is distilled at construction into a frozen scenario object, state is a struct of numpy arrays rather than car objects, and nothing pandas-shaped is reachable from it. That last rule is enforced by a test that spawns a clean subprocess, imports senna.env, and asserts no module containing "pandas" is in sys.modules — the pandas-using strategy extractor is deliberately not re-exported from its own package to keep the import graph clean.

Elsewhere the layering is: fitters are pure functions (frame in, frozen dataclass out, thresholds as arguments, no config reads, no I/O); exactly one module per subsystem touches the database; notebooks contain narrative and call into the package, never the reverse. The five fitting notebooks hold 59 to 107 lines of code each, every computation an import.

4.6Defence in depth against contamination

The 2026 calibration/validation split was frozen on 14 July 2026 — the day the data feature's plan was approved, before any fit existed — and recorded in configs/data_split.yaml with a header stating that editing it afterwards is train-test contamination.

It is enforced at three independent layers, because one forgotten .where clause silently leaks the test set:

  1. SQL — every calibration query appends the round filter.
  2. Runtime — the guard function raises, naming every offending round.
  3. Import boundary — an AST walk over each module asserts that only the two sanctioned modules import the held-out data loader. It is parsed rather than grepped so a comment mentioning the module stays legal, and it asserts the module list is non-empty so a package rename cannot make the check vacuous.

4.7Design the guard so it can actually fire

The subtlest recurring lesson. A check that compares a variable to itself always passes.

When the explainability pipeline writes a surrogate and then builds cards from it, the version guard reads the version back off disk from the manifest it just wrote, rather than passing the version along in a variable. Passing it along would compare a variable to itself — a check that can never fail.

The database-guard tests discover every test module containing a destructive call rather than listing them, because a hard-coded list cannot notice the new module that forgets the guard; a second test asserts the discovery is non-empty so it cannot go vacuous. The evaluation suite's own review found four tests that passed against deliberately broken code — a path tautology, two renders in one process, a hard-coded module list, and a config key nothing read — and each fix carries a canary that the exercise actually happened.

5Subsystems

Each subsystem below follows the same shape: what it is for, the decisions that shaped it and what they rejected, how it is built, and how it is verified.

5.1Data ingestion and the store

Role. A one-time bridge from two public sources into local Postgres, so no later stage ever queries the internet. Every Race and Sprint session from 2022–2026 is fetched once, flagged rather than filtered, written transactionally with a per-session checkpoint, and audited by a committed per-season quality report.

Sources, and one that is unreachable by design. FastF1 is pinned at >=3.7,<4 because 3.7 is the Jolpica-backed line released after the Ergast API shut down in early 2025 (R-04, F-10). The rejection is enforced rather than asserted: a test greps every tracked source file for the dead service's name, assembling the search string at runtime so the guard survives its own scan — the first version failed CI on its own filename. OpenF1 supplies historical pit and stint data from 2023 onward; its real-time tier is paid and 2022 does not exist there, so the client raises before any network call for an out-of-range year (R-05).

Resumability as a transaction property. The checkpoint flips to loaded as the last statement inside the same transaction that writes the rows. A separate mark-loaded step admits a checkpoint that lies in either direction; an in_progress state would then need repairing after a crash. An absent row means never attempted, so there is nothing to reconcile after a kill. This mattered immediately — FastF1's own 500-calls-per-hour budget killed the first bulk run, and nothing was lost.

Two idempotency patterns, split on whether a natural key exists. Data tables delete-and-reload, because race control messages have no natural key: real feeds repeat identical messages and no constraint can identify "the same row". Identity rows (events, sessions, checkpoints) upsert, because the FastF1 pass and the OpenF1 pass share them and each must refresh only the columns it actually knows.

A 404 is data, not an error — at exactly two endpoints. The pit and stint endpoints treat 404 as zero rows; raw fetches still raise so a mistyped path fails loudly. This overturned an earlier decision: the first bulk run excluded nine early-2023 sessions citing "no upstream data", then no-stop sprints returned 404 from one endpoint while the sibling endpoint returned real rows for the same session. The exclusions were reverted, because an exclusion over one empty endpoint would have discarded real data and put a false gap in the quality report.

Implementation. 1,611 lines across ten modules. The orchestrator (ingest.py, 558 lines) owns the resume loop and the single-transaction write; schema.py declares twelve SQLAlchemy 2.0 mapped classes; cleaning.py is the specification's cleaning contract as pure functions with injected thresholds; fastf1_loader.py is the package's only FastF1 import site, with the on-disk cache enabled before any other call; db.py holds the disposability guard; snapshot.py produces the frozen data archive. Alembic migrations rather than create_all, because the second migration had to alter a live schema — adding a dry-race flag to sessions, relaxing a pit-stop column to nullable — against a database already holding three seasons of laps.

Verification. Fifty-plus tests including seven that run against real Postgres transactions (kill-mid-ingest then rerun equals a clean single run; a crash inside the write transaction leaves no partial state), twelve on the wipe guard, and virtual-time tests on the token-bucket throttle. CI runs alembic upgrade head then alembic check so schema drift fails on push.

Outcome. The five committed quality reports (generated 15 July 2026) audit 121,406 laps across 227 checkpointed session loads, with zero missing telemetry channels and zero exclusions. The frozen snapshot taken on 29 July records 123,708 laps across 231 loads — two further 2026 race weekends ingested in both sources between those dates, and therefore not covered by a regenerated report. Quoting either figure is fine; quoting one as the other is not, and the reports would need regenerating to close the gap. Cleaning cost for 2024 as the representative season: 3,259 of 29,023 laps dropped as inaccurate (11.2%), 1,721 in/out laps separated, 2,143 flagged non-green.

5.2Physics fits

Role. Turn the cleaned store plus the telemetry cache into every physics constant the simulator claims: tyre degradation, joint fuel/evolution pace, event hazards, pit loss, energy integrals — 1,765 leaf values, each carrying the notebook that produced it.

The identifiability problem, and reporting it honestly. Fuel burn and track evolution both make laps faster as a race progresses. Fitting either alone books the other's share of the shared downward trend, so they are estimated in one regression (E-10) with session, driver and compound fixed effects.

But constructed fuel inside a single race is exactly affine in lap number, so the two effects have no unique solution there. The fit is therefore per circuit per era, pooled over that circuit's dry races — a recorded divergence from the specification's literal "joint fit per race". When the variance inflation factor exceeds its threshold the code refits without the fuel column, reports the combined trend the data always pins down, and marks the circuit identified: false rather than reporting the arbitrary split the regression produced anyway.

The pooling rule came from a diagnostic worth stating: pooling every weak estimate across the 23 fitted 2024 circuits gave a mass sensitivity of −0.0055 s/kg, meaning a heavier car laps faster. That is a bias signature, not noise. Identification comes from post-interruption laps, which also carry cold tyres, a bunched field and fresh rubber, and the fit books those pace effects to fuel. So the pool takes identified circuits only, inverse-variance weighted. Of 29 circuit-era joint fits, exactly one is identified — Canada 2026, whose sprint weekend gives the pooled fit two sessions of differing race length and enough departure from a straight fuel line to separate the two effects, at a variance inflation factor of 23.6 against a threshold of 30.

Reporting one identified fit out of 29 is a stronger result than reporting 29 confident ones, and the notebook prints both pools side by side so the diagnostic survives.

Degradation without double-counting. The tyre wear curve is fitted on the joint fit's partial residuals — the raw residual with the age terms added back — so the power law cannot re-explain fuel or evolution. The fitter raises rather than filters when handed in/out or inaccurate laps: silently dropping them would hide an upstream contract break, and an in-lap's +20 s sits at stint end where it reads as a tyre cliff, corrupting exactly the coefficient the fitter exists to produce.

Small-sample statistics, chosen for where the data is thinnest. Hazard bands use the Wilson score interval rather than the normal approximation, which collapses to zero width at zero-of-n — exactly where most circuits sit with four or five races. The per-lap hazard conversion carries a boundary shrink, because the first version produced log(0) for a circuit with one race and one safety car, which simulated as a safety car every lap of every race.

Implementation. 1,591 lines across twelve modules, plus five notebooks holding 59–107 code lines each. The orchestrator runs six stages in declaration order because declaration order is dependency order. No statsmodels: variance inflation factors, coefficient standard errors and condition numbers are computed by hand over the same numpy design matrix, which keeps one dependency and one code path.

Verification. A synthetic race with injected coefficients (57 laps, 20 drivers, a safety-car window, staggered stops) proves the estimator recovers what was injected when identification exists, and flags the clean race where it does not. Safety-car laps carry +35 s and in/out laps +20 s deliberately, so a fitter that fails to exclude them cannot pass the recovery tolerance — the filter is tested by consequence, not by inspection.

5.3Circuit ribbons

Role. One self-contained GLB track mesh plus a metadata sidecar per circuit, built from the fastest clean lap's GPS telemetry, with every look-only number flagged stylised.

Frames that do not flip. The ribbon is extruded along rotation-minimising frames computed by double-reflection parallel transport, not Frenet frames. A Frenet normal flips 180° at every inflection and hairpin, and a ribbon edge extruded along a flipping normal turns inside-out (E-17). The test computes the naive Frenet baseline inside the test and asserts it flips before asserting the shipped method does not — the rejected alternative proves itself rejected.

The closed-loop twist residual is measured and spread linearly back over all stations, so the frame is periodic and the start-finish quad carries no seam. Half-width is min(6 m, 0.9 × local corner radius), because Monaco and Hungary have corners tighter than a fixed ribbon is wide and the inner edge would cross the centreline.

Self-intersection is a shipping gate, not just a test. The builder re-runs the intersection check on the built vertices and refuses to export a crossing ribbon, keeping the previously built one instead — a degraded session must not evict a good ribbon.

Elevation and banking are decoration and say so. Smoothed Z exaggerated 1.5×, or flat plus camber where the Z channel is degenerate; both branches stamp stylised into the metadata the UI surfaces, and the dashboard's legend renders that caveat from the flag rather than from hardcoded copy (F-12).

Outcome. Twenty circuits, 2.3 MB of GLB, ~119 KB average. The library is generated from ingested data rather than a hand-listed calendar, so a circuit the season has not yet visited is simply absent — pinned by a test asserting the count is never 22 or 24.

5.4The synthetic 2026 generator

Role. Age the fitted 2024-era physics forward into 2026 dynamics for every circuit, including circuits the 2026 season has not yet visited, through eight named transformations — then grade the result out of sample against real 2026 rounds.

Physics scaling, never a learned generator (R-09). The specification states the rule directly: every transformation is a formula that can be defended line by line. A GAN would have no per-term interpretability and no way to attribute the sim-to-real error to a mechanism. That attribution is exactly what the validation delivered (below).

A grey-box effective car instead of sourced aero constants. Rather than feeding grade-B published aero figures into every lap, a least-squares fit against 2024 telemetry recovers an effective power and an effective drag coefficient from m·a = P/v − βv². Density, frontal area, engine torque and rolling resistance are absorbed into two measured numbers; only the 2026 deltas applied on top are sourced. The straight-line profile is integrated by RK4 over distance with the power taper implemented as specified — full to 290 km/h, linear to zero at 355 — and a closed-form check pins the integrator exactly where drag is zero.

A derived quantity beats a noisy regression. The lift-and-coast cost coefficient is computed by sweeping lift depth on the already-calibrated point-mass model and taking the through-origin slope of extra time against harvested energy. Regressing it off real race laps would be confounded by traffic, tyre state and fuel.

The transformations. T1 powertrain reprofile (straight-line speed, full-throttle time, deployable energy); T2 aero scaling with a fitted corner-speed exponent (0.2239 globally, against the specification's 0.25 fourth-root prior, which survives only as a fallback); T3 mass shift through the fitted mass sensitivity and nowhere else; T4 energy accounting (harvest supply, clipping, deficit); T5 2026 compounds via degradation refit plus cluster transfer; T6 fuel budget; T7 overtaking model refit by hand-rolled Newton-Raphson over 21,159 mined opportunities; T8 residual recalibration against real 2026 calibration rounds.

The result, and it is a failure. Against held-out 2026 rounds 7+, the lap-time Kolmogorov–Smirnov distance is 0.704 (Barcelona), 0.872 (Britain), 0.918 (Austria), 0.946 (Belgium) — 0 of 4 within the 0.15 target. The cause is in the same artifact: simulated laps come out 3.7–6.1 s faster than reality, because in the recomposition step the drag-reduction straight-line gain outweighs the downforce-reduction corner loss. The pooled residual only partly corrects circuits with no calibration data, so the distributions barely overlap.

This is reported as metric M8 with its mechanism attached. The fix — rebalancing the corner-versus-straight physics on calibration circuits only — is named as future work rather than applied, because tuning on the held-out rounds is the contamination the frozen split exists to prevent. A validation that catches a real modelling error is doing its job; the honest response is to say so.

5.5The environment

Role. F1RaceEnv: a 22-car, one-decision-per-lap Gymnasium environment over the fitted physics. A 32-dimensional observation, a MultiDiscrete([6, 4]) action under per-head masks, a three-term reward, and seed discipline strict enough that a subprocess worker is stateless.

Masks, with reasons. Discrete legality lives in the mask (pit lane closed, compound versus track wetness, the two-compound rule, the attack-mode state-of-charge floor, and a guard against pitting onto a compound the circuit has no fit for); continuous legality lives in physics clipping (deployment to what the store and the deployable budget allow, SOC hard-clipped to [0, 4]). Every masked rule carries a reason code that is republished in the step info every lap, because the decision cards must be able to say why an option was off the table, not merely that it was.

No head may ever be left entirely False, since the policy cannot sample from a fully forbidden head. The invariant survives even the forced last-lap pit, because scenario construction refuses a circuit with fewer than two fitted slick compounds.

The unfitted-compound guard, and why it landed before the headline runs. An unfitted slick fell through two disagreeing fallbacks: the dynamics module priced it with the hardest fitted compound's wear curve, while the observation encoder's identity fallback reported grip 1.0 forever. An agent could therefore learn "the soft tyre at Suzuka never degrades" from a fiction. The fix is a mask, not a penalty, even in the penalty ablation — because this is a data limit rather than a sporting rule, and there is no correct price for a tyre that was never fitted. It was recorded as blocking the headline training runs rather than shipped as a hotfix, since it touches the mask contract.

Three reward terms, and the twelve that were removed. Pace shaping scaled per circuit, a potential-based position term, and terminal championship points. The position term is potential-based with the environment's discount at 1.0, so any position path returning to its start sums to exactly zero — which kills the lose-and-regain oscillation pump that the earlier twelve-term design rewarded (F-15).

Those twelve terms are not deleted. reward_v2.py reconstructs them from the specification's own quotes including the pathologies: the asymmetric position term that nets +0.2 on a swap cycle, the bonus that pays for boxing early on barely-worn tyres, the fastest-lap bonus resurrecting a point abolished in 2025. Reproducing the flaws faithfully is what makes the ablation comparison honest.

The deterministic twin, and why same-seed pairing is a fiction. The environment consumes randomness at a policy-dependent rate — a tyre only rolls for failure past its cliff, a stop only draws service time when it happens — so two policies handed the same seed diverge in their events after the first different decision. Paired comparison therefore runs on an EventTrace: one leg records what the environment applied (status, red flags, weather, wetness, failures, rival stops, grid, driver offsets), and both legs replay that frozen trace. Recording what was applied rather than how rivals decided it means a later rewrite of the rival model leaves old twins replaying identically.

On a twin the ego's own tyre failure is recomputed rather than replayed, because the ego may run a completely different strategy — and it is applied as a hard deterministic cap rather than a hazard roll, so an oracle stint cannot win by banking on a lucky non-failure.

Honest simplifications, declared. Overtaking emerges from lap-time differences plus a dirty-air term rather than an explicit pass probability, because a second unfitted probability would be a second thing to defend. Classification is derived from cumulative race time and never swapped in place, which is what makes the FIFO pit lane and double- stacking meaningful.

Verification. 94 tests in the environment package, including two Hypothesis property tests marked slow: ~100,000 random-legal transitions asserting that the executed action was legal under the mask read before stepping, that SOC stays within bounds, and that harvest minus deployment is conserved; plus a second property test at a circuit with only two fitted compounds asserting the unfitted one never appears.

Throughput. 18,477 environment steps per second aggregate across 12 subprocess workers on a 10-core Apple Silicon laptop, measured with the per-step mask fetch on the clock. That prices the 8-million-step training budget at about seven minutes and is what made the three-seed plus four-ablation plan safe to commit to.

5.6Opponents, baselines and the oracle

Role. Everything the agent is measured against.

Rivals vary along their strategic character, drawn per race. Three archetypes — undercutter, tyre-saver, opportunist — differ in pit-trigger tyre age, safety-car response probability, lap-noise scale and slick-compound preference. The safety-car response is the axis E-06 names, and it is applied as a per-car Bernoulli against a per-race drawn probability rather than a shared deterministic rule, so the field's answer to a neutralisation is a distribution rather than a memorisable stampede. Two simplifications are declared: rivals hold balanced energy mode, and archetypes carry no mean pace offset — the noise scale widens the spread without moving the mean — because tying strategy style to car pace would confound the paired-baseline metric.

Train and test bands are disjoint by construction and rejected at parse time if they overlap. The test band is pushed further toward each archetype's character, so the held-out result measures robustness to harder unseen opponents rather than in-distribution generalisation — and the scripted baseline is run under both bands so opponent difficulty is separable from the agent's own gap. Measured: the same heuristic finishes P6.852 under the train band and P6.631 under the test band, a shift of 0.221 places.

Three baselines (A-07): an engineer heuristic mirroring a real pit wall's ladder, a replayed-real strategy that runs the recorded stops of an actual race, and random-legal as the floor. The replay baseline runs balanced energy everywhere and counts its deviations — a 2024 strategy carries no 2026 energy decision, and inventing one would fabricate the exact thing the counterfactual metric measures. When the mask blocks a recorded stop it defers a few laps then snaps to the nearest legal compound and increments a counter that is reported with the result.

The oracle publishes its assumptions. A two-stage dynamic-programming strategist gives the regret metric a ceiling, and its seven assumptions are stated verbatim in the module docstring: full information, rivals do not react, deterministic physics, greedy per-lap energy, fixed start compound, a stop cap with a ranking stage that ignores traffic, no mid-race replanning. An easy oracle flatters the agent, so the limits are published rather than implied. Two subtleties earn their complexity: the ranking stage's pit charge is status-aware, because a flat charge overstates every plan that boxes under a neutralisation and drops genuinely best plans below the shortlist cut; and pit-entry legality is read from the previous lap's wetness, because that is the mask the agent actually faced.

5.7Training

Role. Train the agent with MaskablePPO through the competence-gated curriculum, and leave an auditable trail rather than just weights.

Why MaskablePPO, and why not DQN. Masks are applied inside the action distribution, so illegal actions have probability zero in both rollout and update. DQN was cut for an architectural reason rather than a compute one (R-06): the action space is two independent heads, and the available implementation requires a flat discrete space. Flattening back to the earlier design's twelve entangled actions would undo the very fix that separating the heads exists to make — the audit finding is worded exactly that way ("a flat 12-action space entangles two independent decisions", F-18).

Budgets are ceilings, never schedules (R-12). This is enforced by making the alternative unrepresentable: no gate function takes a step budget. Stage A and Stage B are pure functions of an evaluation history alone; Stage C's stagnation rule does take the current step, because "no improvement for a million steps" is defined in steps — but it still cannot see the ceiling. The budget appears exactly once, as the value passed to the learning call, and a stage that passes its gate logs transition while a stage that runs out logs budget_stop. All three headline runs exited by gate; none by ceiling. The promoted seed passed Stage A at 294,912 steps of a 1.5-million ceiling, the other two at 196,608.

Gates that survived their own review. Stage A's plateau test compares best-of-window against best-before rather than consecutive evaluations, because the naive reading passed a policy still gaining nearly 3% over the window. Its time bar is one-sided: a faster-than- reference agent passes, and an implausibly large beat passes flagged, because the solo reference is near-optimal rather than provably optimal. Stage B pairs on recorded event traces rather than shared seeds, stores the baseline's own twin roll rather than its noisy recording run, and refuses anything but exactly the configured pair count — a silently dropped seed would bias the finish-ahead rate.

One schedule over the summed ceilings. Learning rate and entropy decay are driven by the true global step rather than the framework's per-call progress, which would re-anchor at each stage boundary and jump the learning rate back up exactly when the observation distribution shifts.

A gamma mismatch, quantified rather than hidden. PPO trains at 0.997 while the potential-based shaping telescopes exactly only at 1.0, so the invariance holds approximately with a residual of about (1 − 0.997) × 0.1 × position per lap. The specification wins; the sensitivity runs are wired and were not run, which is stated rather than implied.

Outcome. Three headline seeds scoring 6.355, 6.295 and 6.542 mean validation points; seed 331 promoted as senna-ppo-5e3ddb15d584-C-01769472. Full three-stage wall clock: six to twelve minutes per seed, 25 minutes for the slate.

The slate was trained twice during F18, on the same physics but under two different recorded fingerprints, and every seed returned an identical score at an identical step. So did all eight ablation arms and the era-matched fine-tune: eleven runs, two independent slates, agreement to the last digit of a float. That was not a designed experiment — the second slate exists only because a config edit moved the fingerprint — but it is the strongest determinism evidence the project has.

5.8Ablations

Role. Turn the training pipeline into evidence about its own design choices.

Arms differ only by config bytes. Each arm copies the configuration tree into an overlay directory and writes its environment overrides there, then calls the ordinary training entry point. No arm-specific branch exists inside the training loop, and two arms cannot share a fingerprint. All four run Stage C from scratch at 4 million steps so the first arm is a matched control the others differ from on exactly one axis. Every arm, including the penalty arm, is scored in the default masked environment, so the numbers are comparable — the penalty arm's violations live in its training curves, never in its selection score.

5.9Explainability

Role. Make every decision explainable, with the explanation's own fidelity printed beside it.

The plan that was impossible. The earlier design specified tree-SHAP on the policy network's outputs. Tree explainers do not accept multilayer perceptrons, and the model-agnostic kernel explainer on the evaluation set was priced at roughly a billion forward passes (F-25). That is a different category from slow, which is why the replacement is a redesign rather than a tuning exercise.

Two paths (A-04). Path one distils the policy into per-head gradient-boosted surrogates and runs exact TreeSHAP on those for every decision. Path two runs a gradient explainer on the true network for a 500-decision sample and reports the rank correlation between the two — the integrity statistic that turns the approximation into a stated transparency property. The separation is structural: the tree explainer's only argument is a booster, and exactly one module touches the network's internals.

Distil the unmasked distribution. The surrogate learns the policy's unmasked head distributions, because the mask depends on per-circuit fitted compounds and a last-lap rule that are not recoverable from the observation — so masked targets would be multi-valued across circuits and would cap fidelity at the mask disagreement rate. The mask is recorded alongside and re-applied when the card is built, and fidelity is scored the way a card consumes the surrogate: masked to legal actions, renormalised, then top-1 agreement and mean divergence.

Distil-or-refuse. The specification says to deepen the surrogate before shipping explanations; the code makes that literal. A three-rung ladder is walked shallow to deep and the first rung clearing 0.90 top-1 agreement wins; after the last rung the pipeline raises and nothing partial ships. Both models cleared the first rung.

Two honesty traps in the counterfactual flip probe. The probe sweeps a feature until the policy's call flips, on the true network under a rebuilt mask. Trap one: the mask must be recomputed at every sweep point, because sweeping wetness or state-of-charge crosses mask thresholds and reporting a flip onto an action the mask forbids there would be a lie. Trap two: the sweep must walk outward from the recorded value, because the first implementation scanned from index zero and returned the range floor for any decision sitting high in its range — a true sentence about the sweep and a false statement about the decision.

Ranking flip candidates across all 32 dimensions rather than the card's top five lifted flip-line coverage from 6.2% to 48.2%, because on 89.6% of cards the strongest driver is a one-hot (compound, track status, the override window) that no sweep can cross.

Background matters as much as the explainer. The 1,024-state background is stratified by circuit, race phase and track status with an even share per stratum rather than a proportional draw. Safety-car and virtual-safety-car laps are a few percent of all laps, and a proportional draw would nearly erase exactly the states the interesting pit calls live in — every safety-car call would then be explained relative to an almost entirely green baseline.

Never explain a ghost (E-15). The surrogate is written before a single card exists, and the card builder reads the version back off disk from the manifest it just wrote. A retrain invalidates every card by construction.

Outcome. Headline model: 99.76% top-1 agreement on held-out pit decisions, 98.19% on energy-mode decisions, mean rank correlation 0.548 across 500 cross-checked decisions with all 500 positive and a minimum of 0.222. 14,460 cards across 240 validation races. The era-matched model scores 99.15% and 98.24% with 13,760 cards.

Both surrogates were refitted after F18 and the two moved differently. The era-matched one improved everywhere. The headline one halved both KL divergences while giving up about nine tenths of a point on the mode head's top-1 and falling from 0.687 to 0.548 on the gradient cross-check — a surrogate closer to the policy's distribution that crosses the argmax boundary more often on near-ties, and a cross-check comparing against a different network's gradients. The claim that survives unchanged is that no sampled decision had the two views disagreeing about direction.

5.10Serving

Role. Give the simulator an address: validate and enqueue in the API, simulate in the worker, stream from storage.

Design decisions are covered in §3.2 (the process split, the playback clock, the worker topology). Three more are worth stating.

The cache key gained a scenario digest, recorded as a divergence. The specification's literal key is model version, environment hash and seed — which means Bahrain and Monaco on seed 42 collide into one cached race. The shipped key adds a digest over the scenario fields plus a purpose discriminator, so a counterfactual leg can never satisfy a plain simulation request. The model version and environment hash come from the served triplet's manifest and are never recomputed at serve time, so a retrain cold-starts its own cache namespace by construction rather than by a flush someone must remember (E-15).

A cache hit is checked, never trusted. A queued or running row older than twice the job timeout is treated as a work horse that died, its key deleted, and the run re-queued. Without that, the key answers with a corpse for seven days and the WebSocket waits forever. Concurrent identical requests are resolved by an atomic Redis claim rather than a lock, so double-clicking costs one insert and zero races.

The era registry degrades loudly. Weights without a fitted surrogate are treated as "not baked yet", because a missing surrogate would fail the pairing check mid-job and turn a graceful fallback into a broken endpoint. Until the era-matched surrogate existed, the counterfactual view served the frozen 2026 model and wrote pending-F14 into the run config, which the dashboard rendered as a visible caveat.

Outcome. Twelve REST endpoints plus one WebSocket across eight route modules; 84 tests across the API and serving packages, including a health-latency test that measures the 250 ms threshold directly while a stream is being consumed and a worker drains a full SHAP-per-lap race in-process.

5.11The dashboard

Role. The only part of the system an examiner watches rather than reads. Three tabs — Strategy, Circuit, Analysis — carrying the six specified views as sections (circuit explorer, race replay, energy, tyre surface, live simulation, counterfactual) plus two the restructure added: a post-hoc strategy page and a decisions page.

The tabs are a consequence, not a taste call. 5.10.3 allows one mounted canvas, so the six views could not fold into one scrolling page — the boundary between two canvas sections crossing the viewport would co-mount both. Sections on a rail is what a one-canvas rule permits, and the six pre-restructure paths survive as query-preserving redirects because the demo deep links point at them.

Two constraints shape every file. The frame budget, which in React terms means nothing that updates at 60 fps may be React; and the interpretability contract crossing the browser boundary, which means every number on screen arrived from an API response and the dev overlay traces it back to the response that carried it.

Nothing re-renders at frame rate. Per-frame data mutates through refs, the render loop reads state through a non-subscribing getter, and the transport slider tracks the playhead through a transient subscription that writes the DOM value directly. React renders at lap rate, driven by selectors whose output changes once per lap.

Twenty-two cars in three draw calls. One instanced mesh for bodies with per-instance colour, one for the detail fin, and one quad instanced mesh whose per-instance UV offset picks each car's code out of a single canvas atlas. Draw calls, not triangles, kill a frame budget. DOM labels are capped at four — podium plus selection — with everyone else on instanced sprite text, and the cap is a pure function so a property test can pin it.

One canvas per route, enforced architecturally (F-28). The shell owns no canvas, each canvas view owns exactly one, and the router swaps route elements in a single commit — two canvases would require two mounted routes, which the router cannot do. The counterfactual's split screen is the interesting exception: one canvas behind two pane divs, each pane scissor-rendering its own scene, sharing one camera so any visual difference between halves is strategy rather than viewpoint.

Motion between laps is declared synthesis. The simulator is lap-quantised, so per-lap progress fractions are interpolated with a monotone cubic — Catmull-Rom was rejected because its overshoot renders as a car visibly driving backwards. The caption saying so is exported from one module and rendered verbatim by all three views that use it.

Generic liveries, enforced by test (F-29). Eleven invented team colours, two cars each, codes C01–C22. A test walks every shipped source file against a deny list of team, sponsor and driver marks; it is the one place in the frontend those names may be spelled out.

One palette, and a walk that enforces it. Colour lived in two surfaces that knew nothing about each other: Tailwind utilities in classNames, and inline hexes in the recharts and R3F code — to the point where one view imported a tint out of another view's file to keep two blues in step. All of it now resolves to config/palette.ts, sampled from the reference screenshots by decoding the PNGs and counting dominant colours, and provenance.test.ts walks config/*.ts and fails any export that does not name its origin. That walk scans upward only: a tag written below an export used to satisfy it, which meant a constant could be introduced by nothing at all.

The honesty statements are pinned by test. 5.10.2 gives every view a sentence about what it is not — energy series are modelled, the counterfactual is in-simulator, mid-lap motion is synthesis. Before honesty.test.tsx a restyle could delete any of them and nothing failed.

Outcome. 6,900 lines of TypeScript source and 3,500 of tests, 200 tests across 38 files. The full field renders in 3 draw calls, countable from the three instanced meshes the scene mounts; the whole replay scene was estimated at about 9 against the 150 budget during planning, and real draw-call counts are read from the dev overlay at runtime rather than committed, because the test DOM cannot see them.

The lesson worth carrying out of this subsystem is negative. The restructure passed 200 tests, two subagent reviews and a clean gate while shipping pages nobody could read: a replay of 22 shapes with no timing beside them, a circuit rendered as a two-pixel hairline, a tyre view drawing a surface for a fact a surface cannot show, and a reward panel that never appeared because decision cards number laps from zero and frames from one. jsdom has no layout engine, so a query for the timing tower would have found all 22 rows while nothing was on screen. Automated checks prove behaviour; only looking at the artefact proves communication, and a UI feature needs both.

5.12Evaluation

Role. Roll the frozen agent through the eleven-metric battery on held-out ground — 24 circuits × 20 reserved test seeds against the held-out rival band — and write one provenance-stamped row per metric into a committed store.

A target without its failure mode is not evidence. Every metric carries a gaming note in its configuration that the report prints verbatim: how this number could be made to look good without the underlying behaviour improving. M1's note names weak opponents, which is why the held-out rival band exists and why the scripted baseline is run under both bands. M2's names simulator optimism: the agent optimised the very simulator it is scored in, while real teams optimised reality.

Statistics declared before the roll. The counterfactual metric's claim is an exact paired sign test with the bootstrap interval explicitly labelled descriptive, and the payload carries that label. At ten races the test needs nine wins to clear alpha. Fixing the ten races, the test and the threshold before the first roll is what makes reporting a 4W/5L/1T wash legitimate rather than an invitation to renegotiate the analysis.

Two rolls per race, for two different questions. The live roll is the deployment world (stochastic pace, drawn service times, real failure hazard) and the descriptive metrics report on it, because they ask what the agent actually does. The twin replays those same events with noise off, and the counterfactual metrics branch from it, because they ask what else the agent could have done in the same race. Pricing an alternative against a noisy baseline prices the noise.

Publish the denominator. The safety-car metric only scores windows with at least one informed lap, because the action for a lap is chosen against the mask built at the end of the previous lap — grading an onset-lap stop as a safety-car call credits foresight the agent did not have. Red flags never open a window at all, since a red-flag tyre change is free rather than discounted.

Separating what pooling would hide. Virtual and full safety cars never pool. Pooled, the result reads precision 0.942 and recall 0.167 over 674 windows — one mediocre skill. Separated, it reads as one competent skill (full safety car: precision 0.942, recall 0.308) and one entirely missing one (virtual: zero calls across 300 windows, 134 of them worth taking). The separation is what turns a mediocre number into a specific, actionable finding.

The era confound, measured. Scoring a 2026-physics agent on 2024 races confounds the car with the strategy. A 2-million-step warm-started fine-tune on the 2024 constants scores 6.336 mean points against the frozen 2026 model's 3.802 on identical 2024 banks — so roughly two and a half points of a 2026 agent's apparent 2024 performance is the car (F-24). The counterfactual metric runs on the era-matched model.

Outcome. Thirteen store rows, three figures, a generated chapter, and the results in §6.

5.13Demo, reproducibility and the freeze

Role. Make the system presentable, provable and frozen.

The demo is engineered, not recorded. The first ninety seconds are specified as tightly as an API: a deep link opens mid-race, a decision card slides in with its fidelity badge, one click reaches the counterfactual split, one more reaches the energy view. Making that executable needed a mid-race seek — the deep link's lap and speed parameters ride the WebSocket as a seek after connect and deliberately never enter the request body, because the cache key is the request's fields and forking it would orphan every pre-warmed run.

A cold link blocks until the simulation completes, so a warming script pre-runs the demo simulations; the cache guarantees the warmed run is the run the link resolves to. A Playwright check drives a cold browser to the demo URL and asserts cars on track inside the 15-second budget. It also gave a long-standing open question its harness: a near-empty 3D canvas observed in Chrome weeks earlier. The suspected cause was benign — at 1× only lap 1 exists for the first ninety seconds, so all 22 cars sit stacked at the start line viewed from over a kilometre away — and with the mid-race seek in place the check passes and the scene renders fully. The check is local-only by design, so it stands as a manual gate before recording rather than a CI guarantee.

The reproducibility pack (A-09). Make targets for the whole pipeline, a frozen seed manifest, config hashes in every result row, and a data snapshot. Two pieces are worth noting.

The snapshot is split in half: the archive is gitignored and is designed to ride the release, while its manifest — name, SHA-256, exact byte count, per-table row counts — is committed, so an examiner can verify a downloaded asset offline. Restore routes through the disposability guard because it drops every table first; dump takes no guard, because guarding the safe direction teaches operators to override guards.

make verify proves the chain from a clean clone: the committed chapter regenerates byte-for-byte from the committed store, the provenance chain is internally consistent, and no public page carries a broken link. It runs in CI.

The chain check has two regimes, and getting this right mattered. The first design — recompute the config fingerprint from disk and compare against what each row recorded — would fail by design, because the suite rows copy their hash from the training manifest and the configuration files were legitimately comment-edited afterwards. A from-disk recompute either goes red on honest edits or pressures nobody to ever touch a comment again. So suite rows are checked for internal consistency against the manifests the repository actually serves, plus the one live recompute that is valid (the frozen seed manifest); the two overlay studies hashed their own inputs at run time, so for them the live recompute is the honest check.

The byte guard caught its own authors. During this feature's own review, the committed chapter had drifted one blank line from what the generator emits, because a template edit was hand-applied to the generated document rather than regenerated. The guard the feature added found it.

One methodological note belongs with this table rather than in a footnote. A twin replay is close to deterministic by construction: the trace supplies the events, and TraceSource returns zero lap noise and the fitted pit-service mean with no draw, so that M3's oracle regret stays a clean ceiling. The five seeds per race were therefore never varying pit service, whatever the config comment used to say — they were varying the random driver-pace draw. With that draw replaced by a measurement, nine of the ten races are now identical across all five seeds, and each per-race delta should be read as very nearly a single number rather than as a mean over five independent rolls. The seeds_per_race constant is left at 5 because changing the shape of a published metric is a separate decision from this one. It should be read with the 25 snapped stops the replay baseline needed and with M8: the counterfactual is priced inside a simulator whose sim-to-real error is still far outside target.

6.2What the misses mean

The misses are not independent, and read together they tell one coherent story.

M1 with M9 is the headline finding. The agent beats the engineered baseline by two positions, and part of how it buys them is running tyres past the cliff: 37 failures per hundred races, mean stint 29.4 laps, longest 76. That behaviour is coherent inside the simulator, where a failure is a probabilistic cost the agent has implicitly priced against the track position a longer stint buys — and it is unacceptable on a real pit wall. The evaluation suite exists to surface exactly this, and it did.

M4's bar is unreachable at several circuits by construction. The deployable-energy figure exceeds what the store can physically deliver there, so the metric publishes an attainable ceiling (0.914) beside the raw utilisation (0.649). Against what the battery could actually deliver the agent uses 0.710. Reporting the raw number alone would read as the agent declining to deploy.

M5's virtual safety car result is the most actionable. Zero pit calls across 300 scored windows, of which the afterstate search says 134 were worth taking. The suspected mechanism — a virtual safety car's smaller pit-loss discount never clearing the threshold the policy learned under full safety cars, plus an observation that may not separate the two states sharply enough — is labelled a hypothesis in the write-up, not a finding, because it was not tested.

M6 compares against a ceiling, not a grade. The post-hoc optimum is found by rolling every candidate lap on the frozen trace with full knowledge of what happened; the agent had none of that. It is an information-set asymmetry, and the note says so.

M8's cause is upstream in the synthetic transform (§5.4), and the split forbids fixing it on the evidence.

M2 is an honest wash. Four wins, five losses, one tie against elite human strategists over ten real races. The claim the dissertation can make is precise: SENNA beats a scripted baseline in simulation (M1), and is competitive-but-not-significantly-better against real strategies on ten real races (M2). Not "the agent beats humans".

6.3What the ablations proved

The penalty arm lands 1.8 points below the best arm and half a point below the matched control, with its two seeds straddling that control — the honest reading is that the architectural argument carries the claim and the numbers do not contradict it. The twelve-term reward costs about a point against the matched control and buries the terminal objective (dominance ratio 0.029 against the headline's 2.221). Terminal-only reward matches the headline within noise, which narrows the three-term reward's defensible role to interpretability rather than performance.

6.4The novel result

The luck decomposition: fixing the policy and varying event draws against varying decision noise attributes 75.8% of finishing-position variance to the event draw, with a confidence interval of [0.679, 0.820] across 24 circuits at a mean policy entropy of 0.605 nats. Entangled variance — re-sampled decisions meeting different downstream events — is charged to the decision side, which makes the published figure a conservative lower bound.

This is the answer to a question strategy analytics usually dodges: how much of a result is the strategy at all. Most of it is not.

6.5Sensitivity studies

Two overlay studies price assumptions rather than asserting them.

The harvest cap (E-02). The FIA refined the maximum permitted recharge from 8 to 7 MJ in April 2026. Editing the committed configuration would desync the fingerprint the served model records and invalidate the whole chapter, so the refined figure runs as an eval-only overlay on the same seeds. The frozen agent loses 0.133 places and its derate share rises from 0.078 to 0.182 — it keeps its results and runs out of energy more than twice as often. Framed explicitly as the frozen agent's robustness, not what a model re-optimised for 7 MJ would do.

The borrowed energy coefficients. The 2024 scenario borrows the 2026 energy-mode fit, because no 2024 fit of those coefficients exists. Perturbing the four borrowed values by ±50% moves the counterfactual metric by at most 0.46 places against its own 4.7-place descriptive spread. The borrow is a documented coarseness rather than a load-bearing unknown — which is a claim now backed by a measurement.

7Verification and validation

7.1Testing philosophy

Roughly 486 Python test functions and 155 frontend tests, written before the code they cover. Four kinds do most of the work:

Property tests where the claim is universal. The compliance property drives ~100,000 random-legal transitions asserting legality, energy conservation and state-of-charge bounds. TreeSHAP additivity is checked over generated feature vectors. The label budget is checked across every grid size and selection state.

Tests by consequence rather than inspection. The fitting fixtures inject +35 s onto safety-car laps and +20 s onto in/out laps deliberately, so a fitter that fails to exclude them cannot pass the coefficient-recovery tolerance. The test does not inspect the filter; it makes the filter's absence fatal.

Tests that compute the rejected alternative. The ribbon frame test computes the naive Frenet normal inside the test and asserts it flips before asserting the shipped method does not — so the reason for the design is in the test, not just in a comment.

Meta-tests and canaries. The destructive-database test discovers modules rather than listing them, and a second test asserts the discovery is non-empty. Store and report tests assert that they actually wrote something before asserting properties of what they wrote.

7.2The vacuous-test lesson

The evaluation feature's review found four tests that passed against deliberately broken code: a path assertion true for every input, a determinism check that could not see set-iteration drift, a hard-coded module list that could not notice a new module, and a config key nothing read. A fifth pinned the database guard by pointing the connection at the real store and calling the real drop — red at the right moment, after erasing the store, with no operator opt-in.

The response was a rule rather than five fixes: every test must be able to fail for the reason it exists, and where that is not obvious it carries a canary asserting the exercise happened. The lesson is written up in full, because a dissertation that shows its testing maturing is stronger than one that claims it arrived complete.

7.3Continuous integration

Every push runs, over the whole repository: lint, migration application plus schema-drift check, the fast test suite against ephemeral Postgres and Redis containers, the frontend type check, lint, tests and production build, a grep of the production bundle for Docker-internal hostnames, and make verify.

One local-only gate exists deliberately: the demo browser check needs a warmed stack and a real browser, so it runs by hand before recording rather than in CI.

8Reproducibility and the freeze

8.1Three tiers

What a reader can reproduce depends on what they have.

A clean clone, nothing else. make verify — the results chapter regenerates byte-for-byte from the committed store, the provenance chain checks out, and links resolve. make report rewrites it. No model, no database, no GPU.

Docker. docker compose up --build gives the full stack, because the frozen models, surrogates and track ribbons are committed and baked into the image rather than mounted. make demo-warm then the demo URL.

The full pipeline. Ingest, fit, synth, train, eval, report — hours of local wall clock. Seeds come from the frozen manifest; every result row carries the config hash and model version that produced it. The Postgres snapshot exists with its digest committed; the release that will carry it is the pending freeze step.

The first two tiers were exercised end to end on 6 August 2026 from a fresh clone of the remote with fresh volumes: dependencies installed from the lockfile, make verify green, the chapter regenerated with no diff, the stack built and started, the snapshot restored (123,708 laps and 29,018 decision cards), the demo warmed, and the first-beat browser check passing in 741 ms. One documentation gap surfaced and is not yet closed: the README's reproduce section names no way to build the Python environment, and the working incantation needs a non-obvious index flag because the lockfile pins across two package indexes.

8.2What the tag will pin

The freeze's last step is unexecuted at the time of writing: git tag -l is empty and no release exists. docs/freeze.md is the runbook, and what it specifies is a v1.0.0 annotated tag whose message pins the headline model version, the era-matched model version, the environment config hash, the seed-manifest fingerprint and the snapshot digest together, so "which exact system produced Chapter 7" has one answer — plus a release carrying the snapshot archive.

Everything the tag will name already exists and is checkable today; the tag makes the set citable as one thing. One decision is still open and belongs to the author: if the submission is a self-contained bundle rather than repository access, the snapshot has to travel with the bundle or via university storage rather than a release link.

Two artifacts stay outside the freeze deliberately: the 12 GB telemetry HTTP cache, which rebuilds from the public APIs, and the experiment-tracking history, which is offline-only — the committed run ledger is the record.

9Limitations

Stated plainly, because every one of them is a question an examiner can ask.

The synthetic layer's transform is wrong in a known direction. Simulated 2026 laps run 3.7–6.1 s fast because the straight-line gain outweighs the corner loss. Every downstream result inherits that: the agent is trained and evaluated in a world whose absolute pace is optimistic. Relative comparisons — agent versus baseline in the same simulator — are unaffected, which is why the position-delta metric is the headline claim and the sim-to-real metric is reported honestly beside it.

One identified joint-pace fit out of 29. Fuel and evolution are near-unidentifiable within a race by construction, so mass attribution rests on a pooled coefficient from a single circuit. The pooled value is used everywhere and flagged everywhere it lands.

The counterfactual comparison is in-simulator. The agent optimised the simulator it is scored in; real teams optimised reality. Ten races is a small sample and the analysis says so before reporting the result.

Tyre failure rate is unacceptable for anything but a simulator, and is the clearest target for future work: the failure hazard is priced into the reward only through its consequences, and an explicit risk term is the obvious next experiment.

The agent is blind to virtual safety cars. The mechanism is hypothesised, not established.

Weather has two modes, not three. Both the "mixed" and "wet risk" profiles run the same Markov engine; the requested profile is recorded on the run and echoed by the API so the distinction survives in the data even though the physics does not honour it.

The production deploy is an unverified runbook. Compose is the supported and verified target; the cloud deployment path exists and is documented as never executed.

10Process

10.1One feature, one session, one branch

The build ran as sixteen features, each a single session with a fixed ritual: plan in read-only mode and stop for approval; branch, write tests first and show them failing, implement in vertical slices, run subagent reviews, write a lesson; merge with a non-fast-forward commit and update the index. Tests before code was a hard rule, and every feature's test-first commit precedes its implementation commits in the history.

Two supporting artifacts fell out of this and turned out to matter more than expected. The seventeen lessons in docs/learn/ explain each subsystem in plain language with line-level walkthroughs — they were written for the author's own understanding and became the source material for this document. The plans in docs/plans/ record what each feature intended before it was built, including the divergences it expected, which is what makes the recorded divergence list (Appendix D) trustworthy rather than retrospective.

10.2AI assistance

This project was built with AI assistance, declared per the COMP702 policy. The collaboration was structured rather than ad hoc: the specification was the contract, plans were approved before implementation, tests were written before code, and every diff was reviewed by two independent automated reviewers against the specification and against a code-quality standard before it could land. Several findings in this document — the flip probe scanning from the wrong end, the report's impure figure links, the missing chain check on the newest artifact — were caught by that review battery rather than by the author or by the tests.

The dissertation prose is the author's own. This document is a record of the system, not a substitute for the argument the dissertation has to make about it.
